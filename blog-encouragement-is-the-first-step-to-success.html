Encouragement Is the First Step to Success: Why Stories That Build Confidence Matter for Every Child

Iris Scarfone · 30 June 2026 · 5 min read

Every child experiences moments of uncertainty. Whether it’s taking a first step, learning to read, making new friends, or trying something unfamiliar, childhood is filled with challenges that require courage. While these moments may appear small to adults, they often feel enormous to young children. The difference between giving up and trying again frequently comes down to one powerful factor: encouragement.

Children are not born with unwavering confidence. Instead, confidence develops through experience, patience, and the reassuring presence of adults who believe in them. Every encouraging word, every supportive smile, and every gentle reminder that “you can do it” becomes another building block in a child’s emotional development.

Today’s children grow up in an increasingly fast-paced world where comparisons happen almost everywhere. In school, on playgrounds, and even through digital media, children can easily start comparing themselves to others. Some learn quickly, while others need additional time and practice. Unfortunately, many children mistakenly believe that needing more time makes them less capable.

This is why teaching resilience during early childhood has become more important than ever.

Resilience is not simply about overcoming obstacles; it is about developing the confidence to continue trying despite setbacks. Children who understand that mistakes are a normal part of learning become more willing to embrace challenges rather than avoid them. Rather than fearing failure, they begin viewing each attempt as another opportunity to improve.

Parents and educators have long recognized that one of the most effective ways to teach these values is through storytelling. Stories allow children to witness emotions, challenges, and triumphs through characters they quickly grow to love. Instead of being told what they should do, children observe characters making choices, facing fears, and discovering their own strengths.

This emotional connection makes children’s literature uniquely powerful.

Picture books are particularly valuable because they communicate on multiple levels simultaneously. Simple language introduces important ideas in ways children can understand, while illustrations provide visual cues that deepen emotional understanding. Even children who cannot yet read independently can follow a story through expressive artwork, making picture books ideal tools for early learning.

Rhyming stories offer additional educational benefits. Repetition, rhythm, and predictable language patterns improve listening skills, strengthen vocabulary, and help young readers develop phonemic awareness. Reading rhymes aloud also creates enjoyable shared experiences between children and adults, encouraging positive associations with books from an early age.

Beyond literacy, children’s books also introduce essential character values. Themes like kindness, patience, perseverance, empathy, teamwork, and self-belief become easier to understand when presented through engaging narratives. Rather than feeling like lessons, these values naturally emerge through the experiences of relatable characters.

Animal stories have remained especially popular because they create universal experiences children easily understand. Young readers may not have personally climbed mountains or traveled around the world, but they immediately recognize feelings of fear, excitement, loneliness, determination, and happiness in friendly animal characters. These stories create safe emotional spaces where children can explore important life lessons without feeling overwhelmed.

Among the many themes explored in children’s literature, perseverance continues to stand out as one of the most meaningful. Every child eventually encounters something that feels impossible. Whether learning to write neatly, solve puzzles, swim, or ride a bicycle, progress often comes slowly. Books that celebrate persistence reassure children that struggling is not a sign of weakness, it is simply part of growing.

These timeless ideas are beautifully captured in Three Baby Birdies by Iris Scarfone, with charming illustrations by Ronald Scarfone. This delightful picture book follows three young birds as they prepare for one of the biggest milestones of their lives: learning to fly. While two of the little birdies quickly spread their wings and soar into the sky, one remains behind, unable to leave the nest. Feeling discouraged and sad, the smallest bird begins to lose confidence in himself. Instead of criticizing or rushing him, Momma Bird patiently encourages him to keep trying, while his siblings return to help him discover the courage to fly. Together, their support transforms self-doubt into determination, eventually allowing all three birdies to soar together.

One of the book’s greatest strengths is its gentle approach to teaching emotional resilience. It never suggests that success must happen immediately. Instead, it acknowledges that every child develops at a different pace and that patience is an essential part of learning. This reassuring message helps children understand that struggling with something today does not mean they will struggle forever.

The book’s rhythmic, rhyming language makes it especially appealing to beginning readers. Each page flows naturally, encouraging children to participate during read-aloud sessions while reinforcing language development through repetition and predictable patterns. The story remains engaging without becoming overly complicated, making it suitable for preschoolers, early elementary readers, and family story time alike.

Ronald Scarfone’s illustrations add warmth and personality to every page. The expressive artwork beautifully reflects each bird’s emotions, allowing even the youngest readers to understand the story before they can fully read the words. Together, the text and illustrations create a reading experience that is both entertaining and educational.

Parents will appreciate how naturally the story encourages conversations about confidence, perseverance, and self-belief. Teachers can use the book to introduce classroom discussions about emotional growth, resilience, cooperation, and supporting classmates through challenges. Librarians and caregivers will find it to be a welcome addition to early childhood collections because its message remains timeless and universally relevant.

Perhaps the most memorable aspect of Three Baby Birdies is its reminder that encouragement is never wasted. A few kind words, a little patience, and the willingness to stand beside someone during difficult moments can change how they see themselves forever. Children who feel supported become more willing to explore, learn, and face future challenges with optimism.

In a world where children are often encouraged to achieve quickly, Three Baby Birdies offers something refreshingly different. It celebrates progress instead of perfection, persistence instead of pressure, and kindness instead of comparison. Those values make it far more than a simple picture book, they make it an enduring lesson in confidence, family, and unconditional support.

Heartwarming, beautifully illustrated, and filled with meaningful life lessons, Three Baby Birdies is a wonderful choice for families, classrooms, and libraries alike. It is the kind of story children enjoy for its lovable characters and cheerful rhymes, while adults appreciate its thoughtful message about believing in yourself and encouraging others. Long after the final page is turned, its uplifting lesson continues to inspire readers to spread their own wings with confidence.
